Returns, credit notes and voids

A customer returns something

  1. Find the original invoice: by its number, or by the customer's phone number.
  2. Choose the items and quantities coming back. Part of a line can be returned.
  3. Say what condition each item is in. Items you can sell again go back to sellable stock; damaged ones go to damaged stock, so they are not sold or shown online.
  4. Record how the money goes back: cash, UPI, card, through the original payment, or no refund.

The software makes a credit note for what the customer paid on those items, discount included. Several part returns of one invoice always add up to the invoice exactly. The software records the refund; you pay it out yourself.

Every return needs an invoice

Returns are always made against an invoice, so your GST records stay correct. There is no store credit or exchange voucher yet: for an exchange, take the return and make a new sale.

Voiding an invoice

Only the owner can void an invoice, for example one made by mistake. The original stays on record and the software issues a credit note for whatever has not already been returned. You choose whether the goods are back in the shop and how the money was given back.

Invoices and credit notes can't be edited or deleted. Mistakes are always corrected with a credit note, which is what GST rules expect.
